# The production database stalls (found 30 Oct 2026)

Read-only findings from the shared production project (`xlseqwgyjuqhktrguhyc`, Supabase, ap-south-1, Postgres 17) on
30 Oct 2026, 09:50 to 10:05 UTC, after the Eduwit Scout apply showed nine of Eduwit One's checks in `error`. Nothing
here was caused by that apply or by 0027 the day before: the first startup timeouts date from 27 Oct.

## What happened

Three times in two days every scheduled job of every app failed to start for hours (`cron.job_run_details`,
`return_message = 'job startup timeout'`):

| Window (UTC)                 | Length | Jobs that could not start |
| ---------------------------- | ------ | ------------------------- |
| 28 Oct 21:30 to 29 Oct 02:30 | 5 h    | 7,368                     |
| 29 Oct 15:35 to 18:40        | 3 h    | 4,736                     |
| 29 Oct 20:22 to 30 Oct 02:27 | 6 h    | 6,362                     |

In a calm hour (30 Oct 08:00) the database ran 3,122 scheduled jobs, none failed, average 0.0 s. In a stall hour
(30 Oct 01:00) it ran 2,111, 1,254 failed, average 20 s; the ones that finished took 300 to 374 s (their statement
timeouts). `one-tick` failed 60 of 60 minutes in several of those hours ("job startup timeout", "SSL connection has been
closed unexpectedly", statement timeouts); `one-nightly` failed to start on 29 Oct 21:00 (ONE-03 opened). The
database restarted at 20:16:39 UTC on 29 Oct with no shutdown message in its log ("the database system is starting up"),
which points at memory exhaustion; the third window began six minutes later.

In each window the first failure recorded is `one-tick` with "SSL connection has been closed unexpectedly": a check ran
past `checks.transaction_timeout` (30 s), Postgres terminated the session, and within a minute the startup timeouts
began for every job. Eduwit One is the canary (the strictest timeout), not the trigger: the tick already took 43 to 59 s
in the minutes before, so the database was slow first.

## Why

- The instance is a Supabase Micro: 1 GB of memory, `max_connections` 60, `shared_buffers` 224 MB, `work_mem` 2 MB,
  `cron.max_running_jobs` 32.
- The five apps start about 3,150 scheduled jobs an hour on it (52 a minute): B2B CRM 30 jobs, 2,430 starts an hour
  (several every 30 s); Spark 16 jobs, 390; influencer portal 8, 140; website 3, 121; Eduwit One 2, 61; Witty 1 (plus
  Witty's own calls from n8n, among them `w2_cache_plan` at 15.5 s a call every 15 minutes, the slowest statement on the
  database by mean); Eduwit Scout 3, 6.
- Heavy periodic work on the same instance: `b2b-ml-tick` every 10 minutes with a 15-minute statement timeout (8 s
  average, 249 s worst in three days); a pile of nightlies between 20:40 and 22:15 UTC (`b2b-retention` 20:45,
  `inf-nightly` 20:50, `one-nightly` 21:00, `b2b-reconcile-nightly` 21:07, `witty-blocks-sweep` 21:10,
  `b2c-nightly` and `web_seo_daily` 21:30, `b2b-ml-prune` 21:40, `b2b-cron-prune` 22:15); `call one.tick_job()` itself,
  3.5 s a minute when calm, the largest total since the restart (3,343 s over 570 calls, 5.9 s mean including the stall).
- Once anything slows the database, jobs run for minutes, the 32 pg_cron slots fill, nothing else can start, and
  pg_cron keeps scheduling 52 starts a minute into the jam. The stall holds until load falls (02:30 UTC both nights).
- `cron.job_run_details` holds 669,008 rows (123 MB, ten days); the PLAT and B2C-01 checks read it by `runid`, which is
  indexed, so it is a cost but not the cause.

## What Eduwit One did, and did not

- Detected it: PLAT-01B (startup timeouts, since 27 Oct), PLAT-02 (frequent job silent), PLAT-01, PLAT-01W, PLAT-09
  (slowest query doubled) are open; 174 escalation emails were sent in seven days, none of the P1s acknowledged.
- Did not recover: a check killed three times in a row was parked for good (0020, review F3, D-085), a rule written for
  broken check SQL. Nine checks had not run since 29 Oct evening: B2C-01, PLAT-01, PLAT-01B, PLAT-01W, PLAT-02,
  PLAT-04, B2B-01, CAT-01, ONE-03. The apply script's smoke test now ignores a check killed before its own tick (29 Oct),
  so the Scout apply passed over them. 0028 (D-102) makes parking a six-hour back-off, names parked checks in ONE-01 and
  resets the nine.
- Its external heartbeat (HB-vercel:one) has never been written: the Vercel cron is not configured (Setup item), so an
  hours-long outage of the tick itself is reported only through the other incidents' escalations.

## What to do (owner and the apps' developers; Eduwit One changes none of it)

1. Move the organisation to Supabase's Pro plan (USD 25 a month), then upgrade the compute add-on (Small at least,
   Medium before Witty's waves): a 1 GB instance cannot host five job engines at 52 starts a minute. Found later the
   same day: the organisation is on the FREE plan, so compute cannot be upgraded without Pro, the database turns
   read-only above 500 MB (it held 394 MB on 30 Oct; Eduwit One's own history was its main grower until 0029, D-103),
   there are no daily backups, and leaked-password protection is unavailable. Pro lifts the size limit to 8 GB and
   brings daily backups.
2. B2B CRM: lengthen the 30-second ticks to 60 s or more, move `b2b-ml-tick` to a quiet hour or a daily run, and spread
   the 20:40 to 22:15 UTC nightlies.
3. Acknowledge the open P1 incidents in Eduwit One (the hourly emails stop; the record shows they were seen).
4. Configure the Vercel cron for Eduwit One's heartbeat (Setup).
5. Later, Eduwit One's own weight: B2C-01 and the PLAT checks on `cron.job_run_details` at a 5-minute cadence instead of
   every minute, and `one.results` retention (68 MB).
